## Highest-value leads for the next A reviews

**Abenaki:** Begin with the Fort Odanak community-museum history and archaeology series, Flora Sadie Masta’s Carlisle file, the related linguistic finding aid, and the scholarly migration and French-alliance articles. Follow with the Odanak black-ash gathering, Kiuna’s educational account, and the consultation agreement. Keep Wabanaki-wide, Penobscot, and Wampanoag sources in their stated contexts. Recognition laws, genealogical research, community statements, and participant responses require careful attribution and answer different questions.

**Abihka:** Prioritize Marvin Smith’s 1993 Coosa settlement article, Emanuel Drechsel’s *The Natchez Way*, the Mortar diplomacy biography, and the additional Oxford interpretation. The old Smithsonian Swanton PDF URL needs the [current canonical record](https://repository.si.edu/handle/10088/15540); it is the same work already researched, so the replacement is not another source. Keep varying town names and differing mother-town lists visible.

**Absentee Shawnee:** Prioritize the 1825 treaty, the historical discussion of the 1872 legislation, the 1891 proclamation, tribal language and preservation pages, the Thomas Wildcat Alford article, and Spybuck’s *My Home* record. Check current amendments before using the older constitutional transcription. Preserve the exact date of any membership figure. A repository summary or first page is not a fully read article.

**Acoma:** Begin with the Pueblo’s own rebuilding, modern history, canes, and preservation accounts. Then inspect the NPS historical text, the shield-recovery record, and the water-settlement testimony. Proposed agreements, legal recovery, physical return, and completed implementation need separate evidence. The two Cambridge article links are described as public extracts in the supplied study; their full texts have not thereby been reviewed.
