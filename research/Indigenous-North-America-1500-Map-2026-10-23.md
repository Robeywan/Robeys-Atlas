# Homepage reference map: Indigenous American Nations, c. 16th century

Reviewed 2026-10-23 for placement on the Atlas homepage.

## Selection and attribution

Peter Klumpenhower (Diné) published the second edition of *Indigenous American Nations, c.16th Century* on July 15, 2024. The mapmaker describes it as a map of approximate locations of Indigenous nations' territories around the 1500s CE. His edition notes describe specific corrections to the earlier edition, including distinct Chinook-speaking nations, Cofitachequi and Coosa, and revisions to Ofo, Cree and Piscataway locations. The creator identifies himself as Diné and invites people with corrections and greater mapmaking or historical expertise to improve the work.

Primary creator pages reviewed:

- Edition and correction notes: https://indigamerica.blogspot.com/2024/07/indigenous-american-nations-c16th.html
- Downloads and reuse statement: https://indigamerica.blogspot.com/p/downloads.html
- CC0 deed: https://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/zero/1.0/

The creator states that he releases the map and the geometry he owns into the public domain. The downloads page says the downloadable files may be reproduced and modified for any purpose. This Atlas displays the creator's full-color 2024 web JPG unchanged, provides courtesy credit, links to the primary creator pages, and does not imply endorsement. The file was downloaded from the creator's published web-viewing download on 2026-10-23. It is 8,900 by 5,600 pixels and 10,456,721 bytes. A SHA-256 comparison verified the local display copy is byte-identical to the downloaded original: `EFFB4BE93C19E4A0CEA23B3278F7454128300C34A8F1B9931C79B955B7BA7199`.

## What the map can and cannot show

This is a contemporary reconstruction, not a primary map created in the 1500s and not a single exact snapshot for the year 1500. Its mapmaker describes territories as approximate. He notes that Indigenous territories can overlap without strict borders and says shapes were simplified to improve readability. The broad map includes Alaska and northern Canada through the United States and Mexico with regional insets. It is not exhaustive and does not provide full Greenland or Panama coverage. Missing labels or uncolored areas must not be read as absence of Indigenous peoples.

The map includes its own list of source references, but this project did not independently inspect every item in that embedded bibliography. The update notes show the mapmaker's ongoing corrections and his explicit invitation to further improve completeness and accuracy. The Atlas treats this map as one useful visual orientation, not a boundary authority, official territorial map, or substitute for community-published histories and current sources.

## Placement and interaction

The map appears directly below the Atlas's interactive modern-region map on the homepage. Visitors can zoom with buttons or the mouse wheel, pan by dragging once zoomed, use arrow keys when the image viewer is focused, reset to the full view, and open the unchanged full-resolution image in a separate tab. The separate Smithsonian language-map page and its direct hosted PDF link remain unchanged. This reconstructed map is not added to the archival Historical Map Room and is not counted as an inspected source for any people or profile.
