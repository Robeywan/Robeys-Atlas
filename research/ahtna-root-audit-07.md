## Community Harvest System, 2023–2025

Independently read all **eight PDF pages**, including the title/date/authors, scope/eligibility, administration, seasonal narratives and recommendations. Visually checked both cached original renders **pp.5–6**.

This is a substantial AITRC-authored account of a particular federal administrative program: registration and verification, pooled opportunities, agency coordination, weekly reporting, participants’ feedback and proposed rule changes. It adds a later implementation account distinct from the 2016 framework. It therefore **can count as one substantive source**, even though some quantitative presentation is flawed. It should not be promoted as an independently audited statistical study.

Specific numerical conflicts were confirmed. In 2023, the narrative gives 2 hunting participants of 12 registrants, while the chart says 8.3%; in 2024, the narrative gives 8 of 10 while the chart says 60%. The 2024 81-day total does not yield the reported 13-day average across eight hunters. The 2023 15-day/two-person mean is also imprecisely rendered. None of these rates/averages appears in root’s proposed paragraph. The final annotation and research gap already flag the conflicts; no silent repair is needed.

Page 5 expressly limits these seasonal results to the **federal CHS moose program on federal public lands in Units 11, 12 and 13 during 2023–2025**. It states that Nelchina caribou subsistence hunting was closed during that period. The no-success statement is consistent across all three seasonal narratives and the chart. Root’s wording correctly says the program summaries *report* no successful harvests during that period. It does not claim no Ahtna people harvested moose elsewhere, outside the program, under other regulations or in other years.

Eligibility is described as federally qualified rural residents of the named communities, rather than citizens alone. This matches the report’s explanation of why the earlier citizen-only proposal changed. The paragraph does not offer a detailed present eligibility guide; if such a guide is added later, the report’s additional NPS resident-zone qualification and later regulations would need direct verification.

Access, competition and environmental explanations are attributed to the authors/participants, and regulatory changes remain advocacy rather than established later decisions. No current moose population trend, effect size, hunting season, bag limit or proposal outcome is adopted. Retain these limits during integration.
