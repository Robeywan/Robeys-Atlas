# Nations Atlas — continuing research plan

Updated October 19, 2026, from the project owner's instructions.

## Research standard

Provide **at least 12 excellent, directly relevant sources for each tribe or nation**, and more where useful. Revisit the already expanded profiles to reach this standard. Their current Expanded profile label records progress, not research completion.

Each source must contribute substantive evidence or understanding. Prioritize publications by the relevant nation, community, cultural institution, language program, or Indigenous scholar. Add reliable specialist scholarship, books, oral histories, archival records, and government or museum resources as appropriate. Multiple substantive publications by one community can be valuable; the goal is not 12 different publishers.

Do not count duplicate mirrors, search results, bare homepages, passing mentions, or unread references merely to reach 12. Inspect the actual material, explain its contribution and limits, and tie factual synthesis to specific citations. Record authorship, publisher, publication date where established, access/review date, and relevant page or section references for long works. Link to lawful originals and respect cultural restrictions.

Cover names and identity; homelands; historical change and colonial impacts; language; publicly shared cultural life; governance; contemporary priorities; and relationships to other entries, where the evidence supports those subjects. Identify disagreements and missing perspectives. A dozen links alone does not make a comprehensive account.

The original directory includes peoples, individual governments, umbrella terms, historical names, and aliases. Preserve the access points and explain relationships. Genuine alias entries can share a reading collection where the sources support that relationship. Do not invent distinct governments or force unrelated material into a sparse entry to meet the source minimum. Such entries remain visibly unfinished until the evidence is adequate.

## Later design phase

Once research is mostly complete, undertake a broader design phase. Each tribe's section can have its own carefully considered visual treatment, grounded in that community's documented context and visual traditions. Research suitable imagery, attribution, usage rights, and any cultural access restrictions before selecting assets. Develop the design with the owner rather than assigning generic motifs to entire regions or peoples.

Maintain readable text, accessible navigation, consistent citation controls, and clear source organization across all visual treatments. The current research phase takes priority over these future decorative changes.
