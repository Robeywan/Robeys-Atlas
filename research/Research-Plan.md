## Research standard

Aim for **about twelve excellent, substantive, directly relevant inspected sources per tribe or nation**, with more where useful. The owner clarified on October 20, 2026 that twelve is a target rather than a mandatory floor: five or six excellent sources can be sufficient when a careful search does not find further strong, relevant works. Record the search limits, actual inspection scope, coverage and remaining gaps before judging a smaller collection adequate. Never add weak or unrelated material to reach a count. Existing Expanded profile labels record progress rather than completion.

Each source must contribute substantive evidence or understanding. Prioritize publications by the relevant nation, community, cultural institution, language program, or Indigenous scholar. Add reliable specialist scholarship, books, oral histories, archival records, and government or museum resources as appropriate. Multiple substantive publications by one community can be valuable; the goal is not 12 different publishers.

Do not count duplicate mirrors, search results, bare homepages, passing mentions, or unread references merely to reach a numerical target. Inspect the actual material, explain its contribution and limits, and tie factual synthesis to specific citations. Record authorship, publisher, publication date where established, access/review date, and relevant page or section references for long works. Link to lawful originals and respect cultural restrictions.

Limited access is acceptable. Retain excellent relevant sources requiring subscriptions, accounts, institutional credentials, or library access. Note verified access requirements and what was actually inspected. Distinguish an inspected excerpt from an unread full work; valuable unread references can remain further-reading leads without being counted as completed content checks. A blocked automated request is not by itself evidence of a reader paywall. Use lawful access and preserve the publisher’s stable link.

Cover names and identity; homelands; historical change and colonial impacts; language; publicly shared cultural life; governance; contemporary priorities; and relationships to other entries, where the evidence supports those subjects. Identify disagreements and missing perspectives. A dozen links alone does not make a comprehensive account.

The original directory includes peoples, individual governments, umbrella terms, historical names, and aliases. Preserve the access points and explain relationships. Genuine alias entries can share a reading collection where the sources support that relationship. Do not invent distinct governments or force unrelated material into a sparse entry to reach a source-count target. Assess these entries honestly: a smaller well-researched collection may be adequate for an initial introduction, while unresolved identity or coverage problems remain visibly open. Neither twelve sources nor five or six sources automatically establishes adequacy.

## Educational purpose

Serve college students and general readers with simple navigation, accessible explanations and relevant educational sources. Avoid opinion pieces and unsupported assertions as factual authority. Distinguish documented facts, scholarly interpretation, oral history and a named person’s experience; clearly attribute each. Community voices remain important evidence when their scope and perspective are explained. Aspirations, advocacy and proposals are not completed outcomes. Compare sources and identify disagreements instead of promising that every account is perspective-free.

## Interactive map phase

The eventual atlas should be map-focused, with a prominent North America map offering smooth movement and zoom. The owner’s latest scope, October 20, 2026, uses **broad selectable regions**: individual U.S. states, Canadian provinces and territories, and countries/territories across Mexico, Central America and the Caribbean. Mexico can begin as a country selection. Click Texas or Costa Rica, for example, to see peoples with documented connections, then open their existing directory profiles and sources. A searchable region list/dropdown provides equivalent access, including small islands. City/address search, arbitrary-location lookup and the earlier one-town Kansas pilot are superseded as first-version requirements.

Build a reviewed region-to-profile index, with stable region/profile IDs, community scope, historical/current association type, dates, source/page references, review status and coverage notes. One people may appear in several regions. Distinguish historical homelands/presence, displacement/migration and present-day communities. Modern political borders are navigation aids rather than Indigenous boundaries, and a regional association does not imply presence throughout that whole region. Empty or short results must explain incomplete research rather than imply absence of Indigenous history or promise an exhaustive list. Verify reuse terms and attribution for the administrative boundary dataset and basemap. Preserve keyboard access and an accessible directory/list alternative; avoid sensitive locations. Start a later prototype with a small number of whole regions chosen for evidence readiness; Texas and Costa Rica are the owner’s examples. The map and major redesign remain later phases. No provider or Google integration has been selected. See Interactive-Map-Plan.md and Atlas-Vision.md.
