## Proposed synthesis and audits

### Alabama and Koasati are related languages

Alabama belongs to the Muskogean family and is closely related to Koasati, also called Coushatta. Their relationship should be kept distinct from the shared name of the Texas tribal government. A resource for one language is not automatically a description of the other.

Cites: al-language-support-1.

### A dictionary built into a learning collection

Cora Sylestine’s educator-led dictionary work underlies a continuing learning collection. The 1993 preface credits her initiative and later collaboration with Heather K. Hardy and Timothy Montler. The current Alabama Dictionary Online adds grammatical explanations and teaching materials through a Texas tribal–WOLF Lab partnership. Its June 2025 verb handout moves from introductions to changing verb forms and partner exercises; the noun guide explains how endings mark a noun’s role. The project says speakers approve public recordings and collected material is owned by and shared with the tribe. These resources do not by themselves establish present fluency or teaching outcomes.

Cites: al-language-1.

### Community experience within language research

In a 2024 interview, Ava Silva connects Alabama learning with listening to her grandmother and with the older generation’s painful experiences of punishment at school. Rojas’s article identifies Silva as the project’s community coordinator and a participant in research on verb conjugations. It also describes recordings shared with the community and findings presented to the tribal council. This attributed account helps readers understand the family and community relationships within documentation, while Silva’s experience should not be generalized to every Alabama family.

Cites: al-language-3.

### Questions being investigated in Alabama grammar

Fernandes and Silva’s January 2026 conference presentation examines how Alabama changes verbs to identify participants in an action. They compare earlier placement rules with additional examples and propose that different stages of word formation help explain why agreement markers appear in different positions. Worked derivations and an unresolved question about negation make the talk a substantial research resource. Readers should distinguish the authors’ proposed analysis from established consensus; the presentation offers neither a complete grammar nor a guide to every speaker’s usage.

Cites: al-language-2.

### Making time and materials for learning

Harvard’s September 2026 report describes three June community workshops, including pronunciation, greetings, possession and verb grammar. It also reports weekly online instruction and tribal approval of one paid hour a week for employees to attend. These are concrete, dated actions supporting access to learning. The report does not measure proficiency gains or establish that everyone participates; its value lies in documenting how a particular collaboration was being organized in 2026.
