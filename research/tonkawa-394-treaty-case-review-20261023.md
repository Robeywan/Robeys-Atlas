## Treaty 246, Council Springs (May 15, 1846)

The National Archives / Indigenous Digital Archive viewer and record were reviewed. The record’s title names Longwha (Tonkawa) among ten Native parties and its metadata records ratification on March 8, 1847. The viewer’s transcribed/OCR text was read for pages 2–10, including the preamble, treaty articles 1–14, and signatory lists. The preamble uses “Long-wha”; one signatory list is labeled “Tonkaways.” The treaty text records period U.S. and Native-government terms on trade and intercourse, prisoners, criminal complaints, peace and proposed services. The archive’s normalized named-nation fields omit Tonkawa although its title and scanned text include the group. Separate ratification instruments, implementation, other archival copies and Tonkawa interpretations remain uninspected. No marks were independently identified.

## *Tonkawa Tribe of Oklahoma v. Richards* (corrected Fifth Circuit opinion, February 9, 1996)

Selected sections of the official 20-page opinion were read: case facts; text of the 1864 resolution and 1866 Texas act; later procedural history; the Nonintercourse Act analysis; the panel’s statutory analysis and conclusion. The opinion explains that the Tribe’s suit sought a league of Texas land under the 1866 act. The court affirmed summary judgment for the State, reading the act as requiring the Governor to designate a site and the Tribe to use it as a homeland before a grant could be perfected. The court says this corrected opinion replaces its October 1995 version following rehearing to correct a factual error. This is a narrow disposition of one statutory claim, not a comprehensive homeland account or a statement about present Tonkawa identity. No current legal advice is offered. Underlying legislative and district-court records and later case treatment remain unread.
