## Essential naming finding

Bokota cannot safely be silently merged into Buglé. Some administrative and linguistic references call Bokota an older Buglé label; however, Kevin Evandro Sánchez Saavedra's first-hand 2020 University of Panama essay discusses Bokota/Uga Chere interlocutors asserting a distinct identity, their 2016 assembly and a July 21, 2017 meeting. It critiques the authority given to anthropologists to validate identity for the state. It attributes the proposed name “chengankis” to Pastor Durán's preliminary account, while “uga chere” is the author's account of preferred naming. These are not an authorization to replace everyone's self-designation. Keep original 33, relate original 36, and describe disputed usages.

The 2023 census STILL includes a separate Bokota category. Its national Table 20 records 590 people under that category, 303 men and 287 women. This is a dated census category, not a present membership count or a language-speaker count. Distinguish local northern families from the broad historical linguistic label “Bocotá,” often applied to southern Chiriquí/Costa Rica Buglere speakers.

## Editorial conclusion at pause

A defensible source guide can already cover northern identity and self-identification, state categorization, territory, historical livelihoods and material culture, language knowledge, regional variation, and research ethics. However, achieving 12 by taking every southern linguistic paper would overstate direct northern coverage. Prefer a clear “reviewed-source-guide” status until contemporary community-authored sources and broader balanced evidence are available. Do not label the identity unresolved in a way that makes outside scholarly approval a prerequisite for self-identification. Distinguish uncertainty about the REFERENTS of published labels from community people's authority to name themselves.
