# Tonkawa (original entry 394) — Texas research follow-up, October 23, 2026

This follow-up adds two substantial works to the Tonkawa guide: a scholarly study of the 1862 massacre and an 1866 letter from Texas Governor James W. Throckmorton to the federal Indian commissioner. The profile now has nine substantive inspected works, six supporting references excluded, nine cited sections, and a dated **Still unfinished** assessment. No map was added.

## Sources reviewed

- **Joseph Connole, “A Terrible Truth: The Tonkawa Massacre of 1862,” *Chronicles of Oklahoma* 97(4), Winter 2019–2020, pp. 450–467.** Read the full 18-page article body and selected endnotes through the UNT Gateway to Oklahoma History copy. Connole surveys competing explanations and argues that alliance politics and the failures of U.S. and Confederate protection left the Tonkawa exposed. He notes that immediate instigation remains uncertain and that one first-hand account of the attack on the agency is known. The causal synthesis and reported death estimate remain Connole’s interpretation; most cited records were not independently re-read. The article is scholarly secondary work, not a Tonkawa-authored account. [Article and archive record](https://gateway.okhistory.org/ark:/67531/metadc2017477/).
- **J. W. Throckmorton, Governor of Texas, letter to the U.S. Commissioner of Indian Affairs, September 20, 1866.** Read the full three-page letter in the edited *Indian Papers of Texas and the Southwest*, vol. IV, no. 80, pp. 110–112. The governor described Tonkawa survivors who had returned to Texas and requested food, land, an agent and other assistance. He linked the attack to wartime events and to the Tonkawa’s refusal to join other tribes. His request also situates aid within frontier-security and state-policy concerns. This is evidence of a contemporary governor’s claims and appeal, not the Tonkawa community’s own account, an independently verified casualty count, or proof that proposed aid was delivered. The original Executive Record Book manuscript and federal reply were not inspected. [Letter, starting at printed p. 110](https://archive.texashistorytrust.org/view/49759399/127/).

## Updated coverage and gaps

The new reading path places the scholarly interpretation beside a contemporary government letter while preserving the distinction between community history and external accounts. It does not choose a single cause for the massacre. The profile remains unfinished: direct Tonkawa-authored or community-held public accounts of 1862, more of the underlying archival record, the 1846 Council Springs treaty text and implementation evidence for the 1891 cession remain to be reviewed. No contemporary population figure or map claim was added.
