## 1. George Avery, Archival Investigations of the People of Los Adaes

Public source: https://www.texasbeyondhistory.net/adaes/images/Avery-1996.pdf

Bibliography: *Southern Studies* VII(1), Spring 1996, pp.65–88. The PDF was downloaded normally (HTTP 200), 24 scanned pages. OCR used Windows built-in OCR on rendered pages. Actual reading: printed pp.65–67,70,77,80–85; visual checking of title/page 65,77 and 80. Other pages were screened via OCR; no complete-article reading claim. Page 65 states the study's primary-source and archaeology purpose. The project surveyed translated, transcribed, and original-copy material in multiple repositories; pp.82–85 preserve document references.

Direct contributions: p.77 distinguishes mission religious administration from the surrounding community; p.80 explicitly discusses Adai archival visibility. Avery explains omission of the Adaes from a 1766 Louisiana list of Indigenous fighting men through Texas jurisdiction. This is useful evidence against drawing disappearance conclusions from absence in a particular official list. He interprets the unfilled Adaes settlement icon on Bernardo de Miranda's 1757 map cautiously, explicitly declining to determine whether it meant earlier occupation. The study also explains why identifying Native women from Spanish married surnames alone is difficult. He treats local intermarriage as likely, rather than proving named family genealogies. A Nacogdoches captain's 1757 dispute about French arms trade is evidence about that community, not automatically an Adai event.

Limits/disagreements: p.77 gives 103 Native baptisms by 1768, but the inspected Kress translation of Solís gives 300 entries. Avery's p.77 defines *in articulo mortis* as at the time of death; p.80 loosely says after death. Do not turn this inconsistency into a claim of systematic postmortem baptism. Surnames do not prove a specific ancestry. Bibliographic anomaly: the issue heading is Spring 1996 but notes and bibliography include 1997 and 1999 material; cite the displayed issue while flagging the internal chronology, rather than inventing a later date. No original manuscript images were independently read in this pass.

## 2. Solís inspection diary, 1767–68; Kress translation 1931

Stable public issue: https://archive.org/details/sim_southwestern-historical-quarterly_1931-07_35_1

Relevant page link: https://archive.org/details/sim_southwestern-historical-quarterly_1931-07_35_1/page/n70/mode/1up

Full source title: *Diary of a Visit of Inspection of the Texas Missions Made by Fray Gaspar José de Solís in the Year 1767–68*. Translated by Margaret Kenney Kress, introductory note by Mattie Austin Hatcher. *Southwestern Historical Quarterly*35(1), July 1931, pp.28–76. Public Internet Archive metadata contained no access restriction; normal PDF and OCR downloads returned HTTP 200. Downloaded the complete 103-page issue; read printed pp.28–29 and 61–66, visual verification of 65–66 against page scans. These correspond to PDF 34–35 and 67–72. The original Spanish manuscript, alternate translation, entire diary, and other issue articles were not claimed as read.

Direct contributions: printed pp.63–64 trace the May 1768 trip from Ays territory across the Sabine, via Arroyo Hondo to the Adays mission and presidio, with arrival on May 8. P.65 distinguishes mission and presidio on separate hills across a small plain. It says Indigenous people were numerous and declined congregation at the mission, preferring visits to the presidio. This is directly relevant to choice, mobility, and limits of mission administration; it does not mean a mission village population was the whole Adai population. The same page attributes baptism requests and motives partly to settlers' reports and partly to the inspector's suspicion. The article does not offer direct Adai testimony for those motives.

Disagreement: p.65 clearly prints three hundred baptism entries of children and adults. P.66 continues with 256 presidio baptisms,64 marriages and 116 burials, then different Natchitoches records. This is not Avery's 103. Retain the discrepancy; precise totals require original Spanish manuscript and translation comparison. Neither total is a census. Both the Franciscan author and 1931 editorial framing contain colonial hierarchies and disparaging labels, which must not be adopted as neutral descriptions.

Scope boundary: p.66 changes to the Ays mission. Its captain Urjatana and refusal to congregate are explicitly Ays evidence. Do not conflate Ays/Eyeish and Adai. Earlier Texas/Asinai/Navidacho customs on pp.61–62 likewise cannot be moved into a specifically Adai cultural paragraph.
