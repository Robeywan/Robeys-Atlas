## Resolved primary-record check and useful precision

The February 20, 1875 header and printed pagination match the card. The proposed amendment literally speaks of all bands located on the Alsea and Siletz reservations, while Mitchell’s explanation specifically describes transferring Alsea-reservation residents to the remaining Siletz area. The packet’s broader proposal wording is therefore supportable when labelled as proposal language. The record itself calls the 1865 opening an executive order. It does not support a claim that the reservation residents were exclusively Alsea people.

One useful addition, rather than a correction of a false claim: Sherman objects to compulsory removal without consultation; Allison offers a proviso requiring consent before removal, and the record reports adoption of that proviso and of the amendment as amended in committee proceedings. A concise synthesis can mention the proposed consent safeguard while stating that its adoption does not establish actual community consent, final statutory wording or implementation. Avoid treating the cost debate as the only objection raised.
