# Robey’s Atlas of Indigenous North America — owner’s vision

Recorded October 20, 2026. These are future directions, with research remaining the first phase.

## Purpose and audience

Create an educational atlas that a college student or a general reader can use comfortably. Help someone begin with a familiar place, discover the Indigenous peoples and nations connected to it, and follow clear explanations into reliable sources. Favor simple navigation, readable language, traceable facts and explicit uncertainty. Editorial opinion and unsupported assertions do not serve as factual evidence. Clearly attribute historical interpretation, oral histories and first-person experiences rather than presenting one account as universal fact.

## Research first

Aim for about twelve excellent, substantive, directly relevant inspected sources per collection, with more when useful. The owner expressly allows five or six strong sources when further relevant high-quality works cannot be found. Document the search, actual inspection scope, coverage and remaining questions before judging a smaller collection adequate. A numerical threshold alone does not establish adequacy or complete research. Keep working A first, then alphabetically. There is no rush.

## An atlas centered on the map

The eventual map should become a prominent way to enter the atlas. The owner’s example is: **“I live in Kansas. Who are the Native Americans in my area?”**

The visitor can select Kansas or a general area, zoom toward a town, or search for a place. The map returns an understandable list of relevant peoples and nations, with the dates and meaning of each association. From that list, the visitor opens the existing directory profile, local history and sources. Smooth movement, zooming and exploration should feel natural. The directory remains available for people who prefer names and lists.

Historical homelands, particular past communities, displacement and migration, and present-day communities need distinct evidence and dates. Overlaps are expected. Present state borders are navigation aids rather than automatic Indigenous boundaries. An empty result must explain missing research instead of implying that an area has no Indigenous history. See Interactive-Map-Plan.md.

## Richer authentic imagery across the site

In a later visual phase, make the broad plain background areas around the masthead, navigation and other open spaces feel more connected to the subject. The owner specifically imagines authentic photographs of wampum, powwows, landscapes and cultural life. Use real images with verified reuse terms, captions and attribution. No generated photographs or invented cultural designs.

Identify the particular community, maker, event, place and date where known. A general-site photograph must not imply that its tradition represents every Indigenous nation. Keep navigation, contrast, reading surfaces and performance usable as the imagery grows richer.

## A distinct visual setting for each profile

Each nation’s page should eventually use photographs selected specifically for that community: its people, basketry, food, music, artwork, clothing, landscapes and publicly shared cultural life where appropriate evidence and rights exist. For example, an Abenaki page should use verified Abenaki imagery, rather than generic decoration transferred from another people.

Record the photographer or artist, subject identification, context, date, credit, license or permission, and cultural restrictions. Distinguish historical photographs from present-day life. Public availability alone does not authorize reuse. Exclude restricted or sensitive material; do not turn ceremonial designs into generic ornaments. Preserve substantial readable space for the article and sources even when the surrounding page is visually rich.

## Sequence

1. Establish adequate, inspected, relevant source collections and cited introductions throughout the directory, with A entries first.
2. Develop the map experience and its sourced geographical dataset once the research phase is sufficiently established; the map should guide the later atlas layout.
3. Build the richer shared visual design and individual community treatments alongside that later map/design phase, with authentic, properly credited assets.

The map and major decorative redesign are not being implemented in routine research runs. Suitable public geography and image leads may be recorded while researching. No map provider or Google product integration has been selected.
