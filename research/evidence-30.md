# Biloxi evidence review — October 19, 2026

Prepared for original entry 30. Deliverable: `work/expanded-30.json` (one-record array). Seven substantive sections, 768 words including overview, 13 annotated inspected reading sources. No main website files changed.

The collection distinguishes Biloxi-specific historical/language evidence from sources about the shared contemporary Tunica-Biloxi government. The latter are relevant to descendants’ present political and cultural life, but are not presented as Biloxi-only traditions or a Biloxi language revival program. This is an expanded introduction; the source threshold is not a claim of completion or community review.

## Inspected materials and claim locations

1. **Tunica-Biloxi Tribe, General Profile** — https://www.tunicabiloxi.org/history/ . Read substantive historical text, especially Biloxi encounter on the Pascagoula (web lines 39–40), 1924 authorization for union (52–55), federal recognition (65–66), and contemporary community identities (76–81). Used limited, attributed material. Excluded undated population/acreage counts and did not endorse the suggested sixteenth-century Atuki identification as established fact. Earlier portions are predominantly Tunica history, so the whole page must not be indiscriminately treated as Biloxi history.

2. **Pierite family, Taneks Hanyadi**, established June 1, 2003 — https://indigifab.org/tunica-web/HTML/biloxi.htm . Read the entire short page: identifies named creators, self-designation, historical spellings, linguistic distinction, family memory. Used names and distinction only. A dated speaker-status statement is not a current survey. The page says Emma Jackson was recorded in 1934; this is not evidence that she died in that year.

3. **Dorsey and Swanton, Dictionary (1912)** — original institutional record https://repository.si.edu/handle/10088/15515 ; actual contents and introduction read at https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/A_Dictionary_of_the_Biloxi_and_Ofo_Languages and https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/A_Dictionary_of_the_Biloxi_and_Ofo_Languages/Introduction . The original Smithsonian PDF is 23 MB and web fetch rejected its size; direct local request returned 403. Reviewed the contents and introduction (printed pp. 1–4), not the full 340 pages. Introduction explains unfinished Dorsey material, stem-based reorganization, changed phonetic signs, and unfinished analysis. Counted as **one work**, not separately as a mirror or chapter. Annotation expressly limits review scope.

4. **David V. Kaufman, Reanalysis (2006)** — https://journals.ku.edu/kwpl/article/download/17166/15441/41581 . Full ten-page PDF read through web extraction. Especially pp. 1–2 for family classification, 5 for Swanton’s incomplete symbol reversal, 6–9 for the 21-word comparative method and case-by-case conclusions. Do not reproduce the paper’s unsupported/current-status wording about the “few remaining” Biloxi or assume death in 1934.

5. **David Kaufman, Biloxi Realis and Irrealis Particles (2011)** — https://journals.ku.edu/kwpl/article/download/17443/15718/41858 . Full seven-page article read. Introduction and conclusion frame speaker attitude/certainty, particles, and unresolved gender-related hypotheses. Brief synthesis explains the subject without copying examples or turning a tentative gender analysis into a fixed cultural rule.

6. **David Kaufman, Two Siouan Languages Walk into a Sprachbund (2016)** — https://langsci-press.org/catalog/view/94/121/437-1 . Opened full 24-page chapter. Read pp. 39–42 and 54–59 in detail: distinguishes Siouan inheritance from areal contact; warns that place-name identifications and directions of borrowing are uncertain. Earlier Appalachian connections are presented as the author’s proposal. Institutional full-volume mirror also opened at https://refubium.fu-berlin.de/bitstream/handle/fub188/17797/94-3-428-1-10-20160510.pdf?isAllowed=y&save=y&sequence=1 ; count only the publisher chapter, once.

7. **BIA proposed acknowledgment finding and technical reports (1980)** — https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/GOVPUB-I20-PURL-gpo173801/pdf/GOVPUB-I20-PURL-gpo173801.pdf . Opened 84-page PDF; downloaded to `work/biloxi-sources/recognition1980.pdf` and extracted text to the matching `.txt`. Read recommendation and targeted historical/anthropological sections (PDF pp. 1–3, 27–28, 33, 36–37). Substantive Biloxi material distinguishes Coulee des Grues, Bayou Boeuf/Indian Creek, partial descendants in petitioner, uncertain sequence of moves, and gradual association. Administrative criteria, “remnant” terminology, old counts, and sociological judgments are not adopted as community self-definition. The proposed 1980 dossier does not itself prove the subsequent effective recognition date; tribe’s own history supports 1981.

8. **Megan Biesele, Biloxi Indians**, Handbook of Texas (published 1976, updated June 17, 2020) — https://www.tshaonline.org/handbook/entries/biloxi-indians . Entire substantive entry read. Supports 1828 Neches location, 1839 coercive expulsion context, and 1843/1846 movements. Avoided older speculative “tents in the North” assertion and careless phrase “Sioux language.” This is regional scholarship, not a current community profile.

9. **Bird’s Fort treaty (1843), Texas State Library** — https://www.tsl.texas.gov/treasures/indians/birds-01.html . Read pp. 1–2 and p. 10 at https://www.tsl.texas.gov/treasures/indians/birds-10.html . Proclamation names the Biloxi among parties; signature transcription identifies “Beloxi Chief Hoyo Tubby.” Used that narrow fact and linked the beginning of the complete document. Did not claim to have reviewed every treaty term. This is a diplomatic document involving Biloxi representatives, not merely a passing unrelated mention.

10. **Tribal Language & Culture Revitalization Program** — https://www.tunicabiloxi.org/tribal-info/departments/language-culture-revitalization-program/ . Read program narrative, activities, cultural workshops, camp, and library description. All language instruction on this page is Tunica; no claim of Biloxi curriculum or current course availability. Removed the old entry’s generic annotation.

11. **Tribal Cultural & Educational Resource Center** — https://www.tunicabiloxi.org/cerc/ . Read facilities and visitor information (especially web lines 56–63): exhibit hall, preservation lab, library/research center on the reservation. A practical community institution source; date-dependent prices and hours excluded. Its collections are not assumed exclusively Biloxi. Linked brochure opened but had no extractable text; screenshot output was not inspected, so brochure is **not a separate counted source**.

12. **Lopez, Moore, Morgan, Pierite, Wendte (2026), Hɔhka Moluni**, Journal of Ethnobiology — https://doi.org/10.1177/02780771251411950 . Full text accessible through SAGE. Read authors’ positionality, project methodology, “First Fruits of Our Efforts” including Elisabeth Pierite’s personal account, restoration discussion, and footnote 1. Strong coauthored community/scholarly source. Footnote expressly says the language content is Tunica because reclamation work is further advanced for it. Retain that distinction. Described guidebook is in process, not a completed publication. No medicinal recipe or restricted knowledge reproduced; profile discusses only public account of learning/material access/stewardship.

13. **Tribal Court program description** — https://www.tunicabiloxi.org/tribal-court/ . Entire substantive page read. Supports limited institutional statement about housing, estates, civil cases, and tribal governing documents. No claim that the brief undated page fully states current legal jurisdiction. Court officeholders omitted.
