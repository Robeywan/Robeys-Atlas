# Adai archaeology and exhibit evidence

Content checked October 20, 2026. No source from the earlier five-link profile inherits a content-review date without renewed inspection.

## Texas Beyond History exhibit

Read the complete Cultural Worlds page, the separate Archival Research explanation, Legacy, and Credits. Keep all as one curated exhibit resource. Credits identify Gregory, Avery, Galán, Black, Mariah Wade, and other contributors; they acknowledge Chief Rufus Davis’s assistance. The Adai section directly discusses community settlement and relationships with the mission and presidio. Its cautions about colonial record-making are valuable. Do not reproduce its broad extinction, cultural-hierarchy, or language-classification assertions as current settled facts. Legacy distinguishes the regional Adaeseño Spanish tradition from specific Adai ancestry; do not identify that Spanish dialect as Adaizan. No specialized healing description or sensitive village/burial location is reproduced.

## Webb and Gregory state booklet

Actual 31-page PDF downloaded and text inspected. Cover names the authors and second edition. The foreword says the original was published in 1978 and that the reissue is virtually identical except illustrations; an exact reissue year was not established in the inspected opening, so the source record does not guess one. Read the historical context and the Adaes section, PDF pp. 22–24 (zero-based PDF 21–23). The source has a substantive three-page Adaes discussion, not a passing mention. Its candidate village identifications and artifact associations are historical hypotheses. It conflicts with other sources on some dates and adopts a more confident language classification; keep those differences visible rather than extracting a categorical answer. No specific funerary locations, archaeological site coordinates, or funerary imagery is copied into the profile.

## Gregory, Avery, Lee, Blaine 2004

The publisher-authorized copy is linked by the TBH credits and is a distinct thirteen-page journal article. Actual scan downloaded (2,293,967 bytes). It has no useful text layer. Rendered with bundled Poppler; visually inspected printed pp. 65, 67–71, 74–75. The article concerns the presidio’s wider interethnic economy, Indigenous pottery, French and Spanish goods, architecture, food remains, the differing distribution of artifacts, and descendants’ continuing lives. This is not exclusively an excavation of Adai households. Do not generalize every Caddoan object to an Adai maker or turn an accommodation interpretation into a claim of universal equality. No restricted ceremonial detail is adopted.
