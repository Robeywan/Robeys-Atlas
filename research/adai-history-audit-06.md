# Adai integrated historical and language audit

Reviewed 2026-10-20 against `work/adai-integrated-06.json`, the four inspected historical/linguistic works, and `work/adai-historical-06.md`. No new sources were sought and no integrated or main-site data was edited.

**Result: no material correction required in the audited historical/language passages.**

- **Names and identity:** The introduction places Adaizan among names used in differing historical, linguistic, and contemporary contexts; it does not declare it a separate people or make all name forms mechanically interchangeable. The language/program sections appropriately attribute reconstruction to the contemporary organization. The distinction among Adai people, language, Los Adaes, and current political names remains explicit.
- **Sibley 1805:** The account's Lac Macdon/Bayou Pierre setting and the old establishment to the south are accurately attributed. Replacing exact historical distances with “some distance” introduces no false geographic precision. The figure remains approximately twenty men plus more women, rather than twenty total inhabitants. The source card correctly identifies the actually inspected 1808 Boston reprint and its printed p. 44 / PDF p. 52, distinguishing document date from publication date.
- **Sibley 1807:** The August 13 arrival of parties and chiefs including Adaize is supported by printed p. 50. The integrated text correctly refuses to infer a treaty signature or every later meeting from that entry; the August 18 council list on p. 56 does not name Adai. Other nations' speeches and actions have not been reassigned to Adai. Source metadata correctly identifies the 1922 Abel edition of the separate 1807 journal.
- **Mithun 2017:** The wording preserves her argument that an isolate is suggested, her report of Chafe's assessment, and the documentation-based distinction between an isolate and an insufficiently classifiable language. It neither asserts a demonstrated universal absence of relatives nor treats classification as a decision about living community identity. The source card properly limits its scope and does not claim independent inspection of the manuscript vocabulary or personal communication.
- **Swanton 1942:** The correct Caddo sourcebook title and year are retained. Population-unit cautions, uncertain identity of a later Adaise place, and attribution of the complete-merger claim remain faithful to the selected passages. The unrelated 1911 Lower Mississippi title has not been substituted. No broader Caddo ceremonial or cultural descriptions are generalized into Adai practices.
- **Accounting:** The integrated record contains fifteen sources without an exclusion flag and four explicitly excluded supporting sources. All four historical/linguistic source IDs remain substantively cited. Powell and Gatschet appear as contextual research leads, outside the substantive minimum. This check verifies the accounting structure, not independent inspection of the other contributors' eleven works.

**Optional link convenience:** The `ad-history-2` PDF URL uses `#page=51`, which opens printed p. 49, one page before the key August 13 entry on printed p. 50. Changing only that fragment to `#page=52` would take readers directly to the principal cited entry. The current link still opens the correct work and the annotation supplies the correct page, so this is not a substantive defect.

Current recognition records, bylaws, mission archaeology, and contemporary-program claims beyond their relationship to the historical/language wording were outside this audit's evidentiary scope.
