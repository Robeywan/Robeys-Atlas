## Assessment

The four sources can remain four substantive works **within their stated scopes**. The SIC inventory renews p13-s4 and must not become an additional fifth work through its alternate URL. No material identity or sweeping environmental finding is supported or needed.

**Keep Valtierra’s essay countable as a narrow Indigenous-authored first-person cultural account.** It is a short essay, but its substantive experiential content is more than a generic editorial or passing mention: the author explicitly identifies herself among Amuzgo women weavers, locates the account in her experience in Xochistlahuaca/Suljaa’, describes inherited spinning/dyeing/backstrap-weaving and shared teaching, and explains how she and fellow weavers understand brocading as carrying stories and memory. These are directly relevant, attributed observations and cultural interpretations. They are not independent measurements of historical continuity or environmental performance.

The author’s claims that the processes are sustainable, nonpolluting, regenerative, or uniformly positive for the environment **must not become the Atlas’s factual conclusions**. The proposed paragraph already treats sustainability as her interpretation and expressly excludes a universal environmental-impact conclusion. For an even clearer factual-education presentation, identify the source visibly as “Indigenous-authored first-person essay,” rather than allowing the university host or the broad “Indigenous-led” label to suggest an empirical research paper or a representative statement from every community. Counting it does not require endorsing its environmental argument. No separate count for the uninspected El Sol de Puebla original.
