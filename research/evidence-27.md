# Evidence review — Babine (original entry 27)

Reviewed October 19, 2026. Thirteen substantive reading resources, including community publications, a collaborative legal-history chapter, language materials, and a dated federal implementation announcement. All section claims are linked by source ID in expanded-27.json. Review scopes are recorded individually.

- Who We Are: identity paragraphs 72–73 of the treaty-office page inspected. Avoided treating its older treaty-stage narrative and population estimate as current. A 2024 federal announcement describes a different negotiation history, reinforcing that caution.
- Community Plan 2017–2022: title, introduction printed p.7, community structure and contents, priorities pp.20–26 inspected. No claim that goals were completed. It is a single source, not separate sources for each chapter.
- Fiske–Patrick: UBC Press public eight-page introductory chapter, printed pp.7–14, inspected. It describes research beginning in 1993 and limitations concerning narratives, territorial detail, and language. Full book remains a research gap. Publisher’s full-book listing and sample are not counted twice.
- Harmon: treaty office’s commentary and reproduced 1812 journal passage read. Visitor estimates and colonial language are not adopted as neutral census or identity evidence.
- Barricade dispute: treaty office’s 1906 narrative read. Historical account attributed, without claiming that it substitutes for the original agreement or settled legal interpretation.
- UNBC: October 18, 2012 collaborative excavation report read. Dating belongs to a site. No precise site coordinates or instructions for visiting archaeological places reproduced.
- FirstVoices: public Nedut’en site inspected in the browser after its JavaScript loaded. Alphabet, word-of-day, words/phrases/stories collection, community branding, and contributor/usage structure visible. Public dictionary example also inspected. No restricted access or media reproduction. No audio-based linguistic claim made.
- FPCC: March 31, 2025 feature’s Nedut’en section read. Brian Lacerte/Rosalie MacDonald facts belong to a named pair. Heiltsuk and Okanagan programs elsewhere on the page are not assigned to Babine.
- 2020 update: November publication date, introductory statement, timeline, and FAQ pp.10–11 read. Framework, milestones, and later outcomes separated.
- Governance page: proposed two-level model and ratification steps read. Future-tense description is not a completed governance transition.
- EA page: collaboration versus consent distinction and Nation’s assessment criteria read. No advice about a specific project’s approval or legal obligations.
- Fishery announcement: body establishes July/August 2023 dates; the current homepage still features it. It is not used as current fishery status.
- Federal February 2024 release: body and quoted Lake Babine statement inspected. Funding is a commitment; no assertion that buildings were finished. Avoided conflicting reserve counts and old population estimates.

Rejected/unresolved resources: the full 2020 Foundation Agreement PDF at gov.bc.ca repeatedly failed automated retrieval and was not counted. Generic BCAFN profile duplicates a short orientation and was not retained merely to raise the count. Some older treaty-office archaeology/language links returned verification or gateway errors. The older main Lake Babine history page and timeline give different Fort Kilmaurs dates; no exact establishment date asserted.

The public FirstVoices collection has a dynamic interface. Empty server-rendered text is not evidence that it lacks content; a rendered browser inspection confirmed it. Lake Babine’s main website includes links to a WordPress hosting hostname; retained tested canonical lakebabine.com URLs in this profile.

Editorial cross-check: preferred-name label identifies Lake Babine Nation as the profile focus; Nedut’en is separately searchable. The 2023 closure is explicitly a salmon-fishery closure. The research gaps retain the CCP/treaty-office chronology discrepancy.
