# Ysleta del Sur Pueblo — initial research review

Reviewed October 22, 2026. This bounded Texas-first review verifies a distinct directory access point for Ysleta del Sur Pueblo and builds an initial, source-cited reading guide. The Pueblo is a living sovereign nation and tribal government in the El Paso area. Its own publications identify the community as Tigua. The 1987 Restoration Act redesignated the earlier “Tiwa Indians of Ysleta, Texas” as Ysleta del Sur Pueblo. The access point is distinct from the Pueblo of Isleta in New Mexico, from the wider Tiwa grouping, and from the umbrella term Pueblo.

## Six substantive works inspected

1. **Ysleta del Sur Pueblo, “About Us.”** [Pueblo publication](https://www.ysletadelsurpueblo.org/about-us). Read the full substantive public account on the Tigua community, settlement, agriculture, and current institutions. The Pueblo’s About page and homepage display differing undated member totals; neither is repeated.
2. **Ysleta del Sur Pueblo, *Year-End Report 2025*.** [Pueblo report (PDF)](https://www.ysletadelsurpueblo.org/documents/2025_ydsp_year_end_report). Read selected actual PDF text: the report’s credits and governor letter, governance account, and language/cultural-preservation sections (PDF pages 2–3, 5–6, and 45–50). It is an institutional annual report, not an independent evaluation. The report describes member-controlled archive access; no protected archive material was accessed or reproduced.
3. **Ysleta del Sur Pueblo, *2016 Socioeconomic Profile*.** [Pueblo report (PDF)](https://www.ysletadelsurpueblo.org/documents/2016-socioeconomic-profile). Read the stated planning purpose, historical introduction, methods, and selected geography/workforce tables (PDF pages 12–14, 24–25, 34–35, 38–39). This is a dated community planning study, not a current demographic source; its figures are not presented as current.
4. **Public Law 100-89, *Ysleta del Sur Pueblo and Alabama and Coushatta Indian Tribes of Texas Restoration Act*.** [Statutes at Large, GovInfo](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/statute/101/666). Read the actual statute text in PDF pages 0–3, especially Title I, sections 101–105 and 108. The page records the law as enacted; later amendments and case law were not reviewed.
5. **National Park Service, “The Tiwa of the Isleta Pueblo Mission.”** [Park Service article](https://home.nps.gov/articles/000/tiwa-of-the-isleta-pueblo-mission.htm). Read the substantive article, especially the Pueblo Revolt period, movements to Ysleta and later returns, and archaeological interpretations. This is a federal interpretive account, not a Tigua-authored history.
6. **National Park Service, “Ysleta Mission.”** [Park Service place account](https://www.nps.gov/places/ysleta-mission.htm). Read the full substantive page on the historic site, its lower El Paso Valley setting, and its continuing significance to Tigua people. Its place-based interpretation does not define Pueblo homelands.

## Supporting references, not counted

The full older synthesis is retained for critical comparison. Its population counts differ from other accounts; broad language-status and cultural generalizations are not adopted as current facts.

The page identifies some activities as Tribal-Member-only, and the linked comprehensive preservation plan was not substantively inspected. This concise program page is a useful access point, not a separate full research work.

## Findings and limits

The Pueblo’s own history traces relationships among Quarai, Isleta, and Ysleta through the upheavals surrounding the Pueblo Revolt. National Park Service accounts describe Spanish retreat and relocation while also documenting that some Isletans remained in New Mexico and that some descendants later returned. Older published counts of the people who went south differ. The profile preserves that disagreement and does not adopt a single number or treat the present-day governments as one.

The 1987 law is clear evidence of the legal designation and federal trust restoration it enacted. It is not a current legal opinion or a complete account of later law. The 2016 report is useful for its own time-bounded planning purpose; the 2025 report documents the Pueblo’s stated programs and priorities for that report year. Neither supports unsupported present population claims.

The map index now links the profile to Texas as a **present community/government location only**, based on the Pueblo’s own identification of Ysleta, Texas. This is a navigation route, not a historical-homeland polygon. No historical map was assigned because the relevant source review did not establish a specific map-to-profile connection.

## Initial adequacy

**Adequate initial introduction; research remains open.** Six substantive inspected works give readers a usable entry into the Pueblo’s own identity and contemporary publications, the original federal act, and two place-based federal histories. They support a cited orientation on names, history, government, language work, and place. They do not constitute a full deep dive, community review, current legal analysis, or comprehensive language study.

Further work should seek additional Pueblo-authored public history, carefully compare relevant Spanish colonial records with Tigua accounts and specialist scholarship, and inspect publicly authorized language materials. Current legal implementation, present-day demographic data, and the full community-controlled digital archive were not reviewed. No community endorsement is implied.
