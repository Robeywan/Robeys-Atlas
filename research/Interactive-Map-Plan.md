# Interactive map for Robey’s Atlas of Indigenous North America

Recorded October 19, 2026; revised October 20, 2026 from the owner’s latest direction. The region-based plan below supersedes earlier town-level search, arbitrary-location selection and a one-town Kansas pilot as first-version requirements.

The eventual atlas should be map-focused, with a prominent map that provides a geographical route into the existing directory. Visitors select a familiar modern region and receive a readable list of Indigenous peoples and nations with documented connections to that region, then open their researched profiles and sources. Smooth pan and zoom remain useful, but city/address search, detailed terrain and precise territorial boundaries are not first-version requirements.

## Selectable regions and examples

Use individual U.S. states, Canadian provinces and territories, and countries/territories across Mexico, Central America and the Caribbean as the initial selection units. Mexico can begin as a country-level selection; further subdivisions can be considered later if useful and supported. Small Caribbean countries and territories need readable labels and an equivalent list/dropdown selection. The map must include the directory’s geographical scope, including Alaska and other outlying areas, without sacrificing usable navigation.

The owner’s latest examples are **click Texas → see peoples associated with Texas** and **click Costa Rica → see peoples associated with Costa Rica**. A region opens a contextual list with short explanations, relevant dates, evidence and links to directory profiles. These examples establish the interaction, not an already verified list of peoples or a claim of comprehensive coverage. Modern political borders are navigation aids rather than Indigenous territorial boundaries. A people may appear under several regions where sources support those associations.

## Visitor experience

1. Open a map centered on North America, covering the geographical scope of the directory.
2. Pan and zoom using a mouse, touch gestures, keyboard controls, and clearly labeled buttons. Keep the first version focused on selecting broad regions.
3. Click a state, province, territory or country to highlight it and open a readable list of associated peoples and communities. Offer the same region selection in a searchable list/dropdown.
4. Each result shows a concise identification, the type and period of association, its supporting source and an **Open profile** link to the existing directory URL. Distinguish peoples, governments, umbrella labels and aliases; explain overlaps rather than presenting duplicate access points as separate nations.
5. Offer historical/current association filters if the evidence supports them. Profile pages may link back to documented regions. Later search and globe features can be considered after the broad-region experience works.
6. Retain an accessible list view and the existing directory so the map remains an additional way to explore the research.

## Historical and contemporary context

Separate historical homelands/presence, displacement or migration, and present-day communities/governments through clear result labels and dates. Selecting a whole state or country does not imply that every listed people occupied every part of it. A present administrative seat is a specific location, not the entire homeland. Let cross-border associations show the same people in more than one region where the evidence warrants it.

The core dataset links a stable region ID to one or more existing profile IDs. Each association needs source URLs and page/section references where appropriate, the people/community scope, association type, relevant dates or period, review date/status, coverage notes and reuse terms for any imported material. This is a region-to-profile index; it does not require invented homeland polygons or exact community coordinates. Existing broad homeland descriptions are discovery leads until their regional associations are reviewed. An empty or short result must disclose incomplete research rather than imply no Indigenous presence. Do not label the results as a complete list unless a separate coverage review supports that claim.

Use an appropriately licensed modern administrative-boundary dataset, with provenance and attribution. Check the relevant rights for basemaps and any other imagery before implementation. Sensitive cultural places and private or restricted sites do not belong in the map dataset. No detailed sensitive locations are needed for region-level discovery.

## Preparation during the research phase

Continue researching the entries in alphabetical order, with A entries first and the revised quality-based source target: aim for twelve excellent inspected works, allowing a smaller collection such as five or six when careful research and a documented coverage review support it. When an inspected source provides useful regional evidence, save the supported state/province/territory/country association, time period, scope and citation alongside the profile. Geography research should strengthen articles and the future regional index without delaying the A cohort for detailed boundary drawing.

Source collections across the directory come first; the owner has explicitly allowed this to take weeks or months. The map has been requested as a later feature. It has not been implemented or published. Library, imagery provider, hosting requirements, account access, licensing, costs, and maintenance will be evaluated when implementation begins; no Google product integration has been selected or promised.

## First prototype acceptance

- Smooth pan and zoom work on desktop and touch devices.
- A region-first journey works without knowing a nation’s name: state/province/territory/country, associated peoples, then profiles and sources.
- Begin with a small, carefully sourced selection of whole regions. Texas and Costa Rica are the owner’s examples; pilot-region choices depend on evidence readiness. The earlier single Kansas-town pilot is superseded.
- A people can appear in several regions, and a region can list several peoples, where sources support the relationships.
- Historical and present-day associations are clearly distinguished, with sources and dates visible.
- Profile links preserve existing directory routes; keyboard access, phone/touch behavior, readable small-region selection and the searchable list alternative work.
- Coverage notes explain missing research and avoid implying that a state/country is an Indigenous homeland boundary or that every listed people occupied all of it.
- Review the prototype with the owner before expanding it across the directory or adding more elaborate globe and terrain controls.
