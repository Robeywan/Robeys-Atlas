# Kickapoo (Texas-focused) research review — community policy supplement, October 23, 2026

## Result

The Kickapoo people-level directory entry now includes **14 substantive inspected works**, **6 supporting references excluded from the substantive count**, and **7 cited profile sections**. Its dated initial-adequacy judgment remains **Still unfinished**. This supplement adds two public primary texts from the Kickapoo Traditional Tribe of Texas. They strengthen the record of that government’s stated policies and dated planning process; they do not fill the profile’s broader historical, language, cross-border, or multi-community gaps. No map was added.

## New sources

1. **Kickapoo Traditional Tribe of Texas, *2025 Strategic Tribal Transportation Safety Plan*.** Read the title and contents, vision/introduction, process and safety-partner sections (printed pp. 1–4), and selected education/outreach, data-management, and implementation sections (pp. 34–41). The plan identifies the Tribe as responsible for development and updates, while describing consultant Red Plains Professional’s work with a technical team representing Tribal departments and outside safety partners. It says TxDOT and Kickapoo Tribal Police data were analyzed, while noting that Tribal Police data were less thorough and could only be included in a limited way. The plan proposes school/community outreach and media messages that use or honor Tribal/local language, symbols, or culture. These statements document a 2025 planning process and proposed strategies, not completed actions, demonstrated outcomes, or a general account of Kickapoo culture. The 127-page PDF includes digital appendices and a prior 2015 plan; those were not reviewed. Crash records, precise locations and the linked GIS application were not examined or reproduced. No reuse license was identified.

2. **Kickapoo Traditional Tribe of Texas, *Indian Child Welfare Code*, Chapter 20.** Inspected selected pages of the public 49-page PDF: opening policy and definitions (printed pp. 1–2), jurisdiction and confidentiality (pp. 5–6), and the second policy section (pp. 10–12). The text states policy goals of maintaining children’s connections to family and Tribe and preserving opportunities to learn about culture and heritage. This is evidence of what the posted primary text says, not proof of program outcomes or a complete account of Kickapoo family life or child-rearing traditions. The upload path includes 2017/05, but the code’s enactment/amendment history and current consolidated status were not verified. It is therefore not described as current legal advice. No case-specific or confidential information was used. No reuse license was identified.

## Profile integration

The new section, “Publicly posted policy on family, culture, and community planning,” cites both records and keeps them separate by subject. It presents the child-welfare text as a primary policy source with uncertain currency and the safety plan as a consultant-assisted, Tribe-led planning document with proposed rather than completed strategies. No crash statistics, precise locations, protected records, or map claims were added.

## Quantity and adequacy

The 14 substantive works include multiple scholarly works by Elisabeth Mager Hois; those overlapping assertions are not independent corroboration. Several historical sources are government-centered, edited, or only inspected in selected pages. The two new works expand contemporary primary-source coverage but do not substitute for direct community history or language resources. Initial adequacy remains **Still unfinished**.

Important open gaps include substantive Texas Tribe-authored history and language/education publications; the full 2025 TAMIU thesis (the abstract was inspected, but automated 403 did not establish a reader paywall); verification of the constitution packet and Chapter 20 code’s current status; distinct perspectives from Kickapoo communities in Kansas, Oklahoma and Coahuila; current language-use documentation; reconciliation of contested migration and 1873 event chronology; and community review. These additions establish no new map association.
