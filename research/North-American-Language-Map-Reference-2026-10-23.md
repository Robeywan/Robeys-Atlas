# North American language map reference — October 23, 2026

The owner selected a zoomable map reference for the homepage. The public page at `/language-map/` links directly to the Smithsonian National Museum of the American Indian / Native Knowledge 360° one-page PDF. The PDF names the work **Native Languages and Language Families of North America**, credits Ives Goddard as compiler, and identifies a revised and enlarged edition with additions and corrections from 1999.

The map is a language-family and linguistic-unit reference. Its own accompanying map notes and explanatory Commons documentation describe areas at earliest European contact; contact occurred at different times in different regions, so this is not a single continental snapshot from the year 1500. Areas are approximate; the map does not represent each nation, government, political boundary, or all of Mesoamerica and the Caribbean as individual peoples. A blank area is not proof that Indigenous peoples were absent.

**Source PDF:** <https://americanindian.si.edu/nk360/code-talkers/assets/print/code-talkers-native-languages-map-languages-full.pdf>

**Map metadata:** Ives Goddard, compiler, *Native Languages and Language Families of North America*, revised and enlarged edition (1999). Smithsonian-hosted educational PDF. The live Atlas links to the PDF in the visitor’s browser; it does not download, copy, modify, or republish the PDF. No item-specific reuse license was verified from the PDF itself.

**Content checked:** the actual one-page PDF map and its title, compiler credit, projection, index, and 1999 revision statement were inspected. The map reference page and homepage link include the temporal and category caveats. Review date: 2026-10-23.


**Geographic coverage note:** the map focuses on language areas north of Mexico; it is not a complete representation of Indigenous peoples in Mexico, Central America, or the Caribbean. Its labels and generalized areas should not be treated as nation boundaries.
