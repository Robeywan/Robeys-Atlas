## Newly inspected substantive works

**Anthony K. Webster, “Lipan Apache Placenames of Augustina Zuazua: Some Structural and Discursive Features,” *Names* 55(2), 2007.** The complete 20-page journal article was read. Webster analyzes place names in an account Augustina Zuazua told Harry Hoijer in 1938, including linguistic structure and how names function in the narrative. The article itself cautions against broad claims from a small sample centered on one narrator. Its abstract/main text refer to 22 names while a note describes a twenty-third; some places remain unidentified. This is useful linguistic and ethnohistorical evidence, not a surveyed homeland boundary or a statement of current territorial reach. The article is open through the [American Name Society journal at the University of Pittsburgh](https://ans-names.pitt.edu/ans/article/download/1786/1785/3574).

**Treaty with the Comanche, Ioni, Anadaco, Caddo, Lipan, Longwa, Keechi, Tawakoni, Tonkawa, Wichita, and Waco, Council Springs, May 15, 1846.** The full six-page treaty text reproduced on the Lipan Apache Tribe site was read. It names Lipan among multiple Native parties and contains articles about trade regulation, prisoners, legal arrangements and other matters. It is an instrument framed by U.S. commissioners and named Native parties; the text does not by itself establish implementation, a unified Lipan response, or modern boundaries. The [Indigenous Digital Archive/National Archives catalogue](https://digitreaties.org/treaties/treaty/175516187/) identifies the treaty as Ratified Indian Treaty 246, concluded May 15, 1846 and ratified March 8, 1847. The catalogue’s normalized “Named Native nations” field omits Lipan even though its title and the treaty’s preamble name Lipan; this indexing discrepancy is disclosed rather than resolved by inference. The treaty text is available at the [Lipan Apache Tribe’s treaty archive](https://www.lipanapache.org/LAT/assets/PDFs/treaties/Treaty05-15-1846.pdf).

## Fort Griffin correspondence, 1874–1876 (October 23, 2026)

Read all eight pages of the searchable text of U.S. House H. Ex. Doc. 44-102, *Tonkawa Indians at Fort Griffin, Texas* (Feb. 1, 1876), hosted by GovInfo: https://www.govinfo.gov/app/details/SERIALSET-01689_00_00-020-0102-0000/. It reproduces federal and military correspondence from 1872–1876. Officials report that a small Lipan group moved from Fort Sill to Fort Griffin in 1874; a 1875 Interior Department commissioner describes the group as related to Tonkawa people through intermarriage and considers several possible relocation arrangements. This is an official administrative account and proposal, not a Lipan-authored interpretation, not evidence of a general political merger, and not proof that relocation or consolidation occurred. Do not generalize this particular group's history to all Lipan people or infer incorporation into Mescalero Apache.

The source is cited in the reservation-era discussion and a new Fort Griffin section. The page images, handwriting, signatures, and underlying correspondence files remain uninspected; the OCR contains artifacts. No access restriction was encountered. Lipan has 13 substantive inspected works and seven cited sections, with **Still unfinished** adequacy. No map was added; only the record's named Fort Griffin setting is noted.
