# Atakapa / Ishak — Texas research follow-up, October 23, 2026

This focused follow-up adds one directly relevant, community-authored essay to the Atakapa profile. The collection now contains **11 substantive inspected works**, four supporting records excluded from the count, and six cited sections. Initial adequacy remains **Still unfinished**. This count is distinct from completeness, community review, and endorsement.

## Source reviewed

- **Jeffery U. Darensbourg, “We Are Together Now: Notes on the Film Hoktiwe: Two Poems in Ishakkoy,” *Genealogy* 5(2), article 28 (March 25, 2021).** [Open access article](https://www.mdpi.com/2313-5778/5/2/28) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3390/genealogy5020028). Read the complete two-page article, abstract, acknowledgments, references, and license statement. The journal identifies Darensbourg as an enrolled member of the Atakapa-Ishak Nation. He describes composing poems and songs with examples from David Kaufman’s Ishakkoy dictionary during a 2020 residency and collaborating with Fernando López on the film *Hoktiwe*. This documents one artist’s creative process, not community-wide language use, learning outcomes, or the full content of the film. The article is CC BY 4.0; no poem text, dictionary entries, or film footage is reproduced.

The source is linked to “Ishakkoy documentation and learning” and “Identity, art and cultural memory.” It provides a contemporary, attributed example alongside the Nation’s language resource, Darensbourg’s interview, and his reflective essay about historical memory. These are different works by the same community member and do not amount to independent corroboration of every claim.

## Coverage and remaining work

The guide remains unfinished because historical relationships among groups named in colonial records remain uncertain; French, Spanish, Mexican and mission records need direct review; archaeology must not be used to infer a single present identity; and current community perspectives do not yet represent every organized group or descendant perspective. The complete Kimball grammar, Smithsonian MS 2424 field cards, and Darensbourg–Kaufman language-renewal chapter remain unread. No map was added.

The owner-supplied Jumano study’s 18 URLs were already preserved as reading leads and are unrelated to this Atakapa evidence check. No duplicate extraction or evidence count was created.
