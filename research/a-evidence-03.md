# Absentee Shawnee — evidence and editorial review

Checked 2026-10-19. Draft: `work/a-research-03.json`. Original entry 3 and name preserved. Fourteen distinct resources are used in nine sections; about 1,008 words including the overview. No site files or main profile data were edited.

## Inspected sources

1. **Tribal About Us history** — https://www.astribe.com/about-us . Actual page text inspected. The useful scope is the Tribe's description of movement, political identity and the two communities. Membership figures are dated separately and not repeated. Treat the term “Algonquian dialect” as broad phrasing rather than a technical classification. The health-system history and Oklahoma Historical Society's Absentee Shawnee entry substantially repeat one another; they were read for comparison but are not counted as extra sources.
2. **1854 Shawnee treaty** — https://treaties.okstate.edu/treaties/treaty-with-the-shawnee-1854-0618 . Preamble and Article 2 read in full, with attention to the provisions governing absent Shawnee, the return periods and remaining land or money. This is context for the Absentee distinction, not a claim that every present-day government or dispersed community was a signatory. No genealogy inferred from a signatory's name.
3. **1890 Absentee Shawnee agreement** — https://treaties.okstate.edu/treaties/agreement-with-the-absentee-shawnee-1890-22342 . Articles I–V and signatories read. Agreement date and later congressional ratification distinguished. The clause permitting an agent to allot land when a person failed or refused to select it is especially relevant to the policy's operation. No claim of universal community consent or current jurisdiction drawn from this document alone.
4. **1938 constitution** — https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/GOVPUB-I20-5f3351af2362310ee40455572bcc64fa/pdf/GOVPUB-I20-5f3351af2362310ee40455572bcc64fa.pdf . Seven PDF pages; printed pp. 1–5 inspected, including adoption certification. This is historical evidence of the council/business-committee arrangement, not the present published constitution.
5. **1977 constitution with amendments** — https://www.astribe.com/assets/Constitution.pdf . Fourteen pages. Pp. 1–4, the officer provisions and final ratification certificate inspected. June 4, 1977 ratification and amendments including May 10, 2019 are visible. The published text defines a General Council of adult members, five Executive Committee offices, judicial branch and independent election body. Editorial explanatory notes say they are not operative constitutional language.
6. **January 2026 tribal newspaper** — https://www.absenteeshawneetribe-nsn.gov/assets/newsletter/january_2026_ast_news.pdf . Downloaded successfully, 10,495,518 bytes, ten PDF pages. Front-page article p. 1A inspected using PDF text extraction. It reports the standalone Language/Culture Department and looks back to the December 2022 emergency and alphabet adoption. The 2022 fluency assessment is not a current census. Classes and funding plans are not outcomes. Selected article reviewed; no claim that the full issue was researched.
7. **Tribal lexicon** — https://www.astribe.com/lexicon . Actual tables read: greetings, animals, colors, household phrases, food, months, numbers, seasons and weather. No audio analysis was performed; the presence of audio columns is not evidence that every recording was checked. No unsupported claim that all displayed spelling is identical to the later alphabet decision.
8. **Shawnee Tribe's master-apprentice introduction** — https://shawnee-nsn.gov/news/where-we-speak-shawnee/ . Dated May 6, 2024. Program description and George “Fife” Blanchard's and Daniel Citty's first-person introductions read. Both identify Absentee Shawnee relationships; the program's publisher is the separate Shawnee Tribe. The article is directly relevant through those speakers' contributions, not a transfer of another government's programs to Absentee Shawnee.

## Editorial decisions and open questions

- Preserve the distinction among the Absentee Shawnee Tribe, Eastern Shawnee Tribe and Shawnee Tribe. Link original entry 345 as related, with an explicit note. The Big Jim and White Turkey communities are not treated as separate modern governments.
- Do not repeat the earlier draft's unqualified statement that 1872 gave communal title. Primary agreements, individual allotment rights and later litigation require separate treatment. The draft instead focuses on the directly inspected 1890 agreement and leaves 1872 landholding for deeper review.
- Do not use current membership or fluent-speaker totals from undated summaries. The 2022 survey is described as dated evidence in the annotation, not a present count.
- Earlier newspaper discussion of a podcast is superseded by the much more substantive January 2026 front-page language article. No listening claim is made for an unplayed podcast.
- A 2020 article by John T. Truden on Indigenous activism mentions Big Jim displacement at Lake Thunderbird but the inspected passage is a small part of its wider subject; not included as a quota source. Its cited archival records may help a later community-centered account.
- Do not reproduce ceremonial practices or artworks merely because a source is publicly reachable. The profile gives access to resources and explains their provenance.
