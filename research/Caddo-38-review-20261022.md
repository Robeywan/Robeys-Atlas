## What this collection supports

The synthesis distinguishes Caddo as a living people from the contemporary Caddo Nation of Oklahoma government, the historical Caddo-speaking confederacies, and the narrower Kadohadacho community label used by some sources. The Caddo Nation language program explicitly describes Pawnee, Wichita, Arikara, and Kitsai as linguistically related but separate Nations. The geographic account identifies historical ties to the Red, Ouachita, Sabine, and Neches river regions while retaining uncertainty about the northern Arkansas Basin. It combines archaeological interpretation with attributed Caddo historical writing and testimony, a primary treaty printing, state and federal public history, and a dated constitutional document.

The historical narrative includes Caddo Mounds as one particular civic and ceremonial center, the 1835 treaty’s printed text, Caddo accounts of movement from Louisiana through Texas and westward, and current language and public-history work. The treaty is evidence of the document and its terms, not a complete account of each community’s experience or proof of implementation. Fallis Elkins (1973) and Sadie Bedoka Weller (1968) are named speakers in the oral-history exhibit; their testimony is attributed rather than generalized.

## Remaining gaps

- Inspect more Caddo Nation histories, cultural publications, current government records, and other public community perspectives.
- Deepen the post-removal era, including Civil War, allotment, federal policy, and the 1860–1902 period, using Caddo-centered and archival sources.
- Compare names and histories for the three broad confederacies and their constituent communities without treating changing labels as fixed political borders.
- Inspect the signed 1835 treaty manuscript, ratification records, later agreements, and implementation evidence.
- Review specialist publications and archaeological reports directly, including the unread Caddo Archeology Journal volume.
- Preserve the documented debate about the northern Arkansas Basin and avoid treating archaeological distributions as exact homeland boundaries.
- No present population, speaker count, exhaustive community list, or claim of Caddo community review is made.
